Your Car Has a Logbook. Your Home Doesn't. Here's Why That's Insane.

A shareable thread that makes the case for property data infrastructure using the most intuitive comparison there is.

HT
HomeTruth @HomeTruthUK

Your car has a logbook.

It records every owner, every MOT, every service, every mileage check. You can't sell it without one. Buyers won't touch it without one.

Your home — worth 10x more — has a drawer full of paper and a vague memory of when the boiler was last serviced.

A thread. 🧵

When you buy a used car, you get:

✅ Full service history
✅ MOT record (online, instant)
✅ Mileage verification
✅ Previous owner count
✅ Outstanding finance check
✅ Insurance write-off status

All verified. All digital. All in seconds.

When you buy a house, you get:

❌ No maintenance history
❌ No verified condition record
❌ No central document register
❌ A survey that's a snapshot, not a story
❌ Whatever the seller decides to tell you

The average hidden cost after purchase? £10,000.

It gets worse.

70% of UK homes are underinsured — because nobody has accurate data on what the property actually is, what's been done to it, or what it would cost to rebuild.

Insurers are paying out £1.22 for every £1 they earn.

The whole system is guessing.

Here's the thing: this isn't a technology problem. We solved this for cars decades ago.

The DVLA has a digital record for every vehicle in the UK. MOT history is public. Service records follow the car, not the owner.

We just never built the equivalent for property.

The UK government knows it.

In 2026, they confirmed they're exploring mandatory digital property logbooks. The consultation is underway. It's a matter of when, not if.

EPCs now depend on the paperwork you can provide. No documents = worse rating.

The direction of travel is clear.
